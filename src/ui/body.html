How it works

Each genome is translated in all six reading frames. Every stretch of at least 50 codons between two stop codons is an ORF, whatever the annotation says. Two profile HMMs search all ORFs: one for the INA N-terminal domain and one for the C-terminal cap. The search follows HMMER 3 (local alignment, the same filters and E-values), re-implemented in JavaScript. Scores agree closely, but not exactly, with hmmsearch.

An ORF is an INA candidate if it carries a canonical N-terminal domain or C-terminal cap, or many weaker matches. Canonical means the domain is covered from its start, is significant and sits at the correct end of the protein. ORFs on the same strand within 8 kb form one locus. The start codon is the first Met near the first match, or the annotated start when an annotated gene ends at the same stop codon. When no Met lies near the first match, the bacterial model takes the first Met of the ORF (as in the published analysis) and the fungal model keeps the whole ORF (ORF_start_no_Met). The central 16-aa repeats are tested by their periodicity: identity of 16-aa blocks to their consensus.

Classes: intact, full length: N domain + repeats + C cap in one ORF, ≥ 800 aa; intact, 500–799 aa; intact, no canonical N / C: one terminal domain missing; interrupted: N and C in different ORFs of one locus (a stop codon or frameshift, or a sequencing error); fragment, N only / C only; repeats only and weak repeat match: profile matches but no canonical terminal domain (low confidence).

Contig edge: the column contig edge says when the ORF runs into the end of a contig without a stop codon (5′ or 3′ open). Such loci are often fragments only because the assembly is fragmented.

Exploratory option (off by default): long ORFs without any profile match are also tested for 16-aa repeats. Repeats explained by a period of 4 aa or less (ratio of 4-aa to 16-aa identity above 0.75; INA give about 0.5) are rejected, ORFs overlapping a profile-based locus are skipped, and of overlapping ORFs in different frames only the longest is kept. These calls are hypotheses, not INA genes: see the user guide for the measured false-positive rate.

Limits: the profiles describe the known INA diversity, so proteins unrelated to known INA are not found. Six-frame translation does not splice introns: an intron-containing gene appears as separate fragments or as an interrupted locus. Start codons of unannotated genes are predictions.
